A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Waldorf, MD, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If there is a gas odor, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Waldorf technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
