When to Call for Water Heater Installation

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
