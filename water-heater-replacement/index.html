How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. On the gas side there is the supply line, the sediment trap, the shutoff, and a vent that has to draft properly so exhaust never backs up into the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
