How Hyattsville Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
